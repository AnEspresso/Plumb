If you’re being migrated

CoConstruct is winding down. Buildertrend isn’t the only door.

You didn’t choose CoConstruct because it was the biggest. You chose it because it was built for custom homes, where the client picks every finish. That’s a narrower thing than a general contractor suite, and it’s worth keeping.

Open the example build Talk to me first

To be fair about it: Buildertrend is a genuinely capable product with a real support organisation behind it. If you’re running thirty-plus homes with office staff, it will probably suit you better than this will. This page is for the builders it’s too much for.

What usually goes wrong in the migration

Two things, from what builders describe. The pricing lands differently than expected once it’s tied to annual build volume. And the shape of the product is wider — more modules, more configuration, more surface to learn — which is an asset at scale and a tax when it’s you, a project manager, and a laptop.

If you’re happily on Buildertrend, stay. This is only worth reading if the quote made you wince or the tool feels heavier than the job.

What carries over cleanly

  • Selections. Allowance, real price, the upcharge between, dated client approval. This is the part treated most carefully here.
  • A real homeowner portal. Their house only, photos you chose, decisions you need. Not a read-only PDF.
  • Daily logs with photos that file themselves by date and area.
  • Budgets, signed contracts, payments against them, and a QuickBooks Online export for the paid ones.

What doesn’t

  • Estimating and bidding. Not built. If you estimate in CoConstruct today, you’ll need somewhere else for that.
  • Time tracking and payroll hours. Not built.
  • Bulk data import. Not yet. In-flight jobs come over by hand, which is honest about the work involved. Talk to me before you start and I’ll tell you whether it’s worth it for your situation.

The honest recommendation

Don’t migrate a job that’s halfway through. Put your next build in, run it in parallel for a month, and keep the old system open until you’re bored of checking it. If the homeowner side alone doesn’t save you a conversation a week, walk away and I’ll have learned something useful.
