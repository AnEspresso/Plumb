What you get

Four things, and nothing to learn.

Progress, most days

Photos from site, filed by room. Not a weekly summary written for you — the actual pictures your builder took while working.

They choose what to share. You’ll see finished work, not every scrap pile.

Your selections

Every finish you need to pick, what the allowance covers, and what the one you want actually costs. Approve with one tap.

Dated, so nobody has to remember in August what was agreed in March.

Decisions, before they’re urgent

The things your builder needs from you, with the date they need them by. Answered early, your installs stay on schedule.

This is the single biggest cause of custom home delays. It’s mostly solvable by asking sooner.

Who’s coming this week

Crew days on a calendar, so you know whether Thursday is a good day to walk the site.

If your builder turns that on. Some prefer not to.

Worth being clear about

What you won’t see.

This isn’t a window into your builder’s business, and it shouldn’t be. A good build needs them to have working room.

  • What they pay their subcontractors
  • Their contracts, margins, or job costing
  • Internal notes between them and their trades
  • Photos they haven’t chosen to share
  • Any other house they’re building

You see your house, your money — allowances, upcharges, invoices, what’s outstanding — and the decisions that are yours to make. That’s the line, and it’s deliberate.

Getting in

Two minutes, once.

Your builder sends you a code

It looks like PB-XXXXXXX and usually arrives as a text with a link.

Add it to your home screen

On iPhone: tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. It then behaves like any other app. There’s nothing to download from an app store.

Create your account and paste the code

Your name, your email, a password. The code joins you to your house and nothing else.

Turn on notifications, if you want them

New photos, decisions needed, invoices. Off by default — you’re not going to be pestered.

If your builder isn’t using it

Send them this page.

Plenty of good builders are still running a job out of a camera roll and a group text. It works right up until it doesn’t. If yours would rather see it than read about it, the example build is open to anyone.
