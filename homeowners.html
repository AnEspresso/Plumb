What you get

Four things. Nothing to learn.

Progress, most days

Photos from the site, filed by room. The pictures the builder took that day — not a write-up.

The builder picks what to share. Finished work, not every scrap pile.

Your selections

Every finish you need to pick. What the allowance covers. What the one you want costs. Approve with one tap.

Dated. Nobody has to remember in August what was agreed in March.

Decisions, before they are late

What the builder needs from you, and by when. Answer early and the install stays on the calendar.

This is the main reason custom homes slip. Asking sooner fixes most of it.

Who is on site this week

Crew days on a calendar, so you know if Thursday is a good day to walk the lot.

If the builder turns that on. Some do not.

Worth being clear about

What you will not see.

This is not a look into the builder’s books. It should not be.

  • What the builder pays the crews
  • Contracts, margins, or job costs
  • Notes between the builder and the trades
  • Photos the builder has not shared
  • Any other house under construction

You see your house. Your money — allowances, upcharges, invoices, what is still due. And the choices that are yours. That is the line.

Getting in

Two minutes, once.

The builder sends a code

It looks like PB-XXXXXXX. It usually arrives as a text with a link.

Add it to the home screen

On iPhone: Share, then Add to Home Screen. After that it acts like any other app. Nothing to download from a store.

Make an account and paste the code

Name, email, password. The code joins you to your house and nothing else.

Turn on alerts, if you want them

New photos, decisions needed, invoices. Off until you say so.

If the builder is not on it yet

Send this page.

A lot of good builders still run a job from a camera roll and a group text. That works until it does not. The example house is open to anyone.
