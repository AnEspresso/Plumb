What actually goes wrong

The build is fine. The paper trail is the problem.

Nobody loses a custom home over framing. They lose it over a tile nobody approved and a change order nobody wrote down.

One job, three doors

Everyone sees their own part. Nobody sees your margins.

One site, three sign-ins. Not a permissions screen you configure — it’s the whole design. Subs never see money. Homeowners see only what you share.

Where custom builds actually go wrong

Selections and money, treated seriously.

The two things a spec builder can shrug off and a custom builder cannot. They get the most careful part of the app.

Selections

Every finish with its allowance, real price, and upcharge. One-tap approval, dated. Install specs ride along to the sub who needs them.

Answered early, installs stay on schedule. That’s the whole reason it exists.

Job costing

Budget revisions keep their reason. Contracts you log payments against. Overspend shows amber instead of hiding.

Editing money never rewrites it — the rule the whole money layer is built on.

Built for a basement with no bars

It works with no signal. Not “mostly”.

Everything saves to the device first and syncs when there’s signal. Log twenty items in a poured basement — it catches up on its own.

Synced · live Offline · saved here Saving…

This was tested the hard way

During development a live sync incident wiped a site’s records. Every device still held its own copy, and the whole job republished itself on reconnect. Nothing was lost.

That wasn’t a feature demo. It was a bad afternoon that the design happened to survive.

Why it’s different

I watched a very good builder run my build — and realized how much one person is expected to hold in his head.

A custom build is a logistics problem wearing a hard hat, coordinated largely from memory, a phone, and a truck cab. The craft was never the hard part. The information is.

SitePlumb was written during that build. The homeowner experience isn’t a module bolted on — it’s where this started, and the builder side grew around what a builder actually juggles.

That’s also why the daily log takes four taps. Anything slower doesn’t survive a real jobsite.

Read this before you get excited

Who this isn’t for, yet.

You’ll find this out in week one anyway. Better you hear it from me than discover it after migrating a job.

Estimating and takeoff not built

There’s no bid or proposal side. If you buy software mainly to win work, this isn’t it yet. Budgets start once you’ve got the job.

Crew time tracking not built

No timesheets, no clock-in, no payroll hours. Plenty of tools do this well and they’re cheap.

Subs still need chasing be realistic

The sub view is genuinely narrow and useful. Some subs will still ignore it and answer their phone instead. That’s the trade, not the software.

One person builds it early

Support is me, and I answer. That means fast fixes and honest answers, and it also means no 24-hour call centre. Judge accordingly.
