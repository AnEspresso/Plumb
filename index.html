Where custom builds actually go wrong

Selections and money, treated seriously.

These are the two things a spec builder can shrug off and a custom builder cannot. They get the most careful part of the app.

Selections

Every finish, with its allowance, its real price, and the upcharge in between. The homeowner approves in one tap and the approval is dated. Install specs ride along to the sub who needs them.

Answered early, installs stay on schedule. That’s the whole reason it exists.

Job costing

Budget lines with revisions that keep their reason. Signed contracts you log payments against. Overspend shows amber rather than hiding. Change orders keep their history instead of overwriting it.

Editing money never rewrites it — the rule the whole money layer is built on.

Built for a basement with no bars

It works with no signal. Not “mostly”.

Everything you do is saved on the device first and syncs when there’s something to sync to. Photos, notes, costs, approvals. Walk into a poured basement, log twenty items, come back up — it catches up without being asked.

This was tested the hard way

During development a live sync incident wiped a site’s records. Every device still held its own copy, and the whole job republished itself on reconnect. Nothing was lost.

That wasn’t a feature demo. It was a bad afternoon that the design happened to survive.

Why it’s different

I didn’t build this for builders. I built it because I was the homeowner, and I couldn’t find out what was happening at my own house.

Every other tool in this category was built by contractors, for contractors. The client portal was added later, because someone asked for it.

Plumb was written during a real custom build, by the person standing on the wrong side of the information. The homeowner experience isn’t a module bolted on — it’s the thing that came first, and the builder side grew around it.

That’s also why the daily log takes four taps. It was used every day on a live jobsite before it was shown to anyone.

Read this before you get excited

Who this isn’t for, yet.

You’ll find this out in week one anyway. Better you hear it from me than discover it after migrating a job.

Estimating and takeoff not built

There’s no bid or proposal side. If you buy software mainly to win work, this isn’t it yet. Budgets start once you’ve got the job.

Crew time tracking not built

No timesheets, no clock-in, no payroll hours. Plenty of tools do this well and they’re cheap.

Subs still need chasing be realistic

The sub view is genuinely narrow and useful. Some subs will still ignore it and answer their phone instead. That’s the trade, not the software.

One person builds it early

Support is me, and I answer. That means fast fixes and honest answers, and it also means no 24-hour call centre. Judge accordingly.
